Kundalini is the most unusual class on the timetable and the one people are most likely to have opinions about before trying. In practice it is straightforward: repeated simple movements, strong rhythmic breathing, some sound, and a long rest. Noah teaches it plainly and explains what each part is for.

## The structure

A short tuning-in, a breathing practice, then a kriya, which is a set sequence of movements held or repeated for a fixed time. The class closes with a relaxation and a few minutes of meditation.

## Why the repetition

Doing one movement for three minutes is a different experience from doing thirty movements once each. It gets past the part of your mind that likes variety and reliably shifts energy and mood, which is why so many people book it for a Friday afternoon.

### Come knowing that

- You can stop at any point and simply breathe.
- Chanting is offered, never required.
- Rapid breathing practices are always optional.
- White or loose clothing is traditional and entirely optional too.
- The effects tend to show up afterwards rather than during.

Open to all levels, no flexibility needed, and worth trying twice before you decide what you think.
