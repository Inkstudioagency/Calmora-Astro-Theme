Yin is the slowest thing on the timetable and, for a lot of people, the hardest. You take a small number of floor shapes, support them well and stay in each one for three to five minutes without adjusting every few seconds. What changes over that time is not muscular; it is connective tissue, and it does not hurry.

## What the long holds do

Muscle responds to load in seconds. Fascia and joint capsule respond to sustained, gentle tension over minutes. Yin is built for the second kind of tissue, which is why the shapes look passive and feel like work anyway. The hips, lower back and shoulders are the usual targets.

## The mental half

Staying still is the other practice here. Three minutes is long enough for your mind to get bored, negotiate and eventually settle, and most regulars come as much for that sequence of events as for the stretch.

### How to practise it well

- Find the edge, not the end. Sixty per cent is plenty.
- Use every prop you can reach.
- Stay still once you have arrived.
- Come out slowly, and give yourself a breath before the next shape.
- Keep warm; long holds cool the body quickly.

Suitable for complete beginners and often the class that experienced practitioners find they need most. Bring socks and something with sleeves.
