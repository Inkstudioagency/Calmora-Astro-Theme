Some people find it hard to drop straight into a three-minute hold with a busy head. Yin flow solves that by moving first: twenty minutes of gentle, warming sequence, then forty minutes of long, supported floor shapes. By the time you stop, stopping is possible.

## The first twenty minutes

Slow and undemanding, mostly standing and on hands and knees, with the aim of warming the joints and using up a little nervous energy. It is not a workout and is not meant to be.

## The last forty

Three to five shapes, held for three to five minutes each, targeting the hips, hamstrings and spine. Noah gives a brief anatomical note at the start of each one and then leaves the room to get on with it.

### How to get the most from it

- Take less depth than you think you can manage.
- Stay still once you have settled.
- Notice the last minute of each hold; that is where the change is.
- Keep breathing slowly through the nose.
- Wear layers, and bring socks.

Suitable for everyone, including complete beginners, and a good class to pair with a strong practice earlier in the week.
