Almost everyone meets this in their first month. The wrists ache in downward dog, burn in plank, and the usual conclusion is that they are weak. They are rarely the problem. The wrist is simply where a badly distributed load ends up.

## What is actually happening

In downward dog the wrist sits near the end of its available extension, which is a normal place for it to be. Pain arrives when all of your weight arrives with it: hips low, hands too close to the feet, shoulders not taking their share. The joint is not being damaged so much as asked to do a job that belongs to the whole arm.

## Where the load should go instead

Spread the hand and press through the base of the index finger and the pad below the thumb, so the palm makes a tripod rather than collapsing into the heel of the hand. Then push the floor away until the shoulder blades feel wide. Done properly the arms carry the pose and the wrists merely transmit it.

## Four adjustments to try this week

- Spread the fingers wide and press the whole hand down, not just the heel.
- Move the hands a little further forward, so the shoulders are not stacked over them.
- Lift the hips higher and bend the knees; length matters more than straight legs.
- Push the floor away actively rather than hanging in the shoulders.
- Come down to forearms for a few breaths whenever it builds.

## When it is worth asking someone

If the pain is sharp, sits on the little-finger side, includes pins and needles, or lingers for hours after class, stop working around it and get it looked at. That pattern is worth a professional opinion rather than a better hand position.

Everything else usually resolves within a few weeks. Tell your teacher; there is a modification for every shape here, and using one is not a step backwards.
