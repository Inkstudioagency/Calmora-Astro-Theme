Everyone has them: in bed by eleven, wide awake at one, and increasingly annoyed about it. Breathing practices will not cure insomnia, but they will stop you making a bad night worse, and one of them is simple enough to do half asleep.

## The practice, in full

Lie on your back or your side, whichever you actually sleep in. Breathe in through the nose for a count of four, then out for a count of six or eight, letting the exhale be slow rather than forced. Keep going for ten rounds and then stop counting. If you lose track, you were probably drifting off, which is the point.

## Why a long exhale works

Heart rate rises slightly on an inhale and falls on an exhale. Lengthening the exhale nudges the parasympathetic system, the branch that slows things down, and it gives an anxious mind a small, dull job. Neither effect is dramatic. Together they are usually enough to stop the escalation that keeps people awake.

## What helps it work

- Do it lying in the position you actually sleep in, not sitting upright.
- Breathe quietly through the nose; effort defeats the purpose.
- Keep the exhale slow rather than long; never empty the lungs forcefully.
- Stop counting once it feels automatic.
- Use it when you wake at three as well as when you go to bed.

## The honest limits

Breathing will not fix a bedroom that is too warm, a late espresso, a phone at midnight or a genuine sleep disorder. If you are regularly awake for hours, snoring heavily or exhausted despite eight hours in bed, that is worth a conversation with a doctor rather than a longer exhale.

Within those limits, this is the most useful thing to have to hand at two in the morning. It costs nothing, needs no equipment, and works about as well on the fiftieth night as the first.
