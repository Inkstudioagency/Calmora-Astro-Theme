Breathing practices are the most portable thing yoga has to offer. They need no mat, no room and no particular clothing, and they work in the twelve minutes between a difficult meeting and the next one. Here are the three worth learning first.

## One: the extended exhale

Breathe in for a count of four, out for a count of six. That is the entire practice. A longer exhale nudges the parasympathetic nervous system, which is the branch responsible for slowing your heart rate and settling your stomach. Five minutes is enough to feel it; ten is better. If six feels like a stretch, use five, and never force the end of the breath.

## Two: box breathing

In for four, hold for four, out for four, hold for four. Box breathing is more alerting than the extended exhale and it gives a busy mind something to do, which is why it is popular with people who find sitting still unbearable. Use it before something demanding rather than before bed.

## Three: alternate nostril breathing

Close the right nostril with the thumb, breathe in through the left; close the left, breathe out through the right; in through the right, out through the left. That is one round. Ten rounds is a practice. It is fiddly for the first week and then becomes automatic, and it is the one most people end up keeping.

## How to actually build the habit

- Pick one practice, not three.
- Attach it to something you already do daily.
- Five minutes beats twenty minutes you never do.
- Sit upright, on a chair is fine.
- Stop if you feel light-headed; that is a sign to breathe less, not more.

## A note of caution

If you are pregnant, managing high blood pressure or living with a respiratory condition, keep to the extended exhale and skip the retentions in box breathing. And if a practice makes you anxious rather than calmer, that is worth knowing rather than pushing through; tell a teacher and try a different one.

Start tonight, with five minutes and the first technique. The whole point of these is that they are small enough to actually do.
