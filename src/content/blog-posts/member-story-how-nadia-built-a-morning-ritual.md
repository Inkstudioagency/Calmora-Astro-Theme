Nadia has been at the 7am class most Mondays for two years. She is also, by her own description, not a morning person, has never been especially flexible, and gave up on yoga twice before this. We asked her how the third attempt went differently.

## The first two attempts

“Both times I tried to do too much. Four classes a week, all of them hard, and I would keep it up for a month and then something would happen at work and the whole thing would collapse. And once it collapsed I felt bad about it, which made going back harder.”

## What changed

“A teacher told me to pick one class and defend it, and let everything else be optional. So I picked Monday at seven, because if I miss it the week has already gone wrong and I know about it by nine in the morning. Everything else I do when I feel like it, which is usually once or twice more.”

## The bit either side of the class

“The practice is an hour but the ritual is about two. I walk in, which takes twenty minutes and is when I think. Then class. Then I get coffee and sit for a bit before work. If I drove and rushed off afterwards it would be a completely different thing and I do not think I would still be doing it.”

## What she would tell someone starting

- Pick one class a week and treat it as non-negotiable.
- Make everything else optional so missing it costs nothing.
- Protect the half hour after class as much as the class.
- Go back after a gap without explaining yourself to anyone.
- Expect to be bad at it for a while; that is not a sign.

Two years in, Nadia is still not especially flexible and says she does not much care. “I sleep better, I am nicer to people on Mondays, and I have somewhere to be at seven. That was the whole point.”
