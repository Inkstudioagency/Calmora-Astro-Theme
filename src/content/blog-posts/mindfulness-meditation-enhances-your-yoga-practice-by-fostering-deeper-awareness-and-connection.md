Yoga and meditation are usually sold separately, which is a fairly recent invention. The postures were originally a way of making a body comfortable enough to sit in for a long time. Putting the two back together changes both, and it does not take much.

## What changes on the mat

The first thing most people notice is that they stop leaving. A ten-minute sitting practice trains you to notice when attention has wandered and come back without making a fuss about it, and that skill transfers directly into a flow class. You spend more of the hour actually in the room.

## The second change is slower

After a few months, the relationship to difficulty shifts. A hard shape stops being something to survive and becomes something to observe, which sounds like a small distinction and is not. It is also the point at which the practice starts affecting the rest of the week rather than just the hour.

## How to begin without making it a project

Ten minutes, once a day, in a chair. Follow the breath, count to ten, start again when you lose count. That is the practice, and it is complete; there is nothing more advanced waiting for you later.

- Sit upright but not rigid. A chair is entirely fine.
- Set a timer so you are not checking the clock.
- Losing the count is the practice, not a failure at it.
- Same time each day beats a longer sit at random.
- Ten minutes daily is worth more than an hour on Sundays.

## If sitting still is unbearable

Some people cannot go straight to stillness, and there is nothing wrong with that. Move first: a few minutes of slow, simple movement, then sit. That is exactly how our Meditative Yoga class is built, and it is the reason it works for people who have tried and failed at meditation apps for years.

Start with ten minutes tomorrow. Then do it again the day after, badly, and keep going. That unremarkable repetition is the entire method.
