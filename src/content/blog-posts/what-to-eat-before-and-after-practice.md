The advice usually given here is either vague or unnecessarily strict. In practice most people need two things: a rough sense of timing, and a short list of what tends to sit well. Everything beyond that is personal.

## Timing is most of it

A full meal wants two to three hours before a strong class, mostly because twisting and folding on a full stomach is unpleasant rather than dangerous. A small snack wants forty-five minutes to an hour. For a gentle or restorative class none of this matters much; you can eat an hour before a yin class and be perfectly comfortable.

## Before a morning class

Plenty of people practise at 7am on nothing but water and feel fine. If you do not, half a banana, a few dates or a spoon of nut butter forty minutes ahead is usually enough. The aim is something small and easy, not a meal.

## Afterwards

Within an hour or so, eat something with both protein and carbohydrate. After a strong class this matters; after a restorative one it does not much. Rehydrating properly is more important than most people think, especially in the warmer rooms.

## What tends to sit well

- Before: banana, dates, oats, a small yoghurt, toast.
- After: eggs, lentils, rice and vegetables, a smoothie with protein.
- Any time: water, before you are thirsty.
- Best avoided immediately before: anything very fatty, very large or very fizzy.
- Worth testing: coffee before an early class. It helps some people considerably and unsettles others.

## And the rules you can ignore

You do not need to practise fasted, you do not need a special diet to do yoga well, and there is no food that will improve your practice by itself. Eat enough, eat at sensible times, and pay attention to what your own stomach thinks. That is genuinely the whole of it.
